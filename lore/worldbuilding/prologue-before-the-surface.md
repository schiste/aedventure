# Prologue: Before the Surface

> **Status:** `draft for canon review`
>
> **Scope:** The world and events experienced by the player before the Hero's first step onto the surface.
>
> **Date:** 311 AS
>
> **Boundary:** This document ends at the threshold of the Bresme caves. It does not describe the cousins' deaths, Studio Echo, the Crystal, the Hero's Resilient status, or any event after the first step outside.

## Narrative purpose

The prologue must establish Les Grottes de la Bresme as three things at once:

1. a successful refuge that preserved human life;
2. a functioning society with families, work, rituals and conflicts;
3. a settlement that has reached the limits of its current space.

The Hero does not leave an abstract bunker. They leave a home, a social order and a family reputation that already shape the mission before the player makes any later choice.

The prologue is therefore not an exposition sequence. It is a compressed passage through an inhabited world.

---

## Canonical starting state

At the beginning of the prologue:

- the year is **311 AS**;
- Les Grottes de la Bresme contains approximately **2,000 people**;
- the settlement is close to its sustainable carrying capacity;
- the caves remain the community's primary protection from the Filter;
- Hush-2 still requires sustained sound for most infected humans;
- the ordinary surface survival window remains **4 in-world hours**;
- the community has no confirmed knowledge that the Hero is Resilient;
- Studio Echo is still a family legend and an unverified surface lead;
- no Crystal has been confirmed at the destination;
- the Lindquist family is associated with dangerous missions and the cult of "The Studio";
- Sylvain Marchand is the Dadi and supports investigating the new lead.

These are the facts available to the community. Later revelations must not be retroactively placed in the Hero's knowledge before the expedition.

## 1. A day in the caves

### Spatial structure

The settlement is organised by depth and inherited family quarters:

- **Entrance Hall, 5 m:** checkpoint, inspection and controlled access;
- **Wine Cathedral, 15 m:** large communal gatherings;
- **La Salle Piaf, 20 m:** music, ceremonies and the Marchand collection;
- **Mushroom Garden, 30 m:** food production;
- **Spring Chamber, 35 m:** water collection;
- **The Sanctuary, 45 m:** primary residence and family life;
- **Deep Refuge, 55 m:** emergency shelter;
- **The Echo Room, 25 m:** forbidden acoustic hazard.

The depth hierarchy is practical, cultural and political. The deeper chambers are safer, but they are also more valuable and more contested.

### Work

Every family contributes to a rotating set of obligations:

- water collection and inspection;
- mushroom and underground food production;
- ventilation and drainage maintenance;
- tool repair and salvage processing;
- sound continuity and drum shifts;
- medical care and birth support;
- teaching and archive preservation;
- entrance watch and expedition preparation.

A person's status is visible through the work they are trusted to perform. The Lindquists are repeatedly assigned dangerous tasks, while their contribution to planning remains limited.

### Sound and silence

Sound is not a general protection against the Filter. It is used inside the settlement to manage Hush-2 and preserve the community's mental and physical stability.

The cave therefore has strict sound customs:

- drum rotations must be maintained;
- unnecessary noise near entrances is forbidden;
- music is permitted only in controlled chambers;
- the volume boundary in La Salle Piaf is enforced;
- the forbidden Echo Room is never entered;
- silence is treated as a medical and social danger, not as peace.

Children learn these rules before they understand their history.

### Food, water and space

The Bresme settlement survives through a combination of:

- underground springs;
- cultivated fungi and mushrooms;
- stored and preserved food;
- seeds and limited underground agriculture;
- recovered supplies from surface expeditions;
- strict allocation through families.

The immediate crisis is not a lack of all resources. It is the inability to expand production and housing at the same rate as the population.

The pressure is experienced as:

- crowded family chambers;
- disputes over rooms released by deaths;
- young couples waiting for space;
- reduced privacy;
- greater dependence on dangerous expeditions;
- arguments over whether the settlement should expand deeper or reach outward.

## 2. Families and belonging

Everyone belongs to a family. Families provide:

- housing;
- food allocation;
- education;
- protection;
- representation on the Council;
- responsibility for burial and remembrance.

A person without a recognised family is almost inconceivable. This makes family reputation a material force, not merely a cultural detail.

The Council has approximately one representative per family. The Dadi coordinates the settlement, but every decision is filtered through family interests.

The Hero enters the prologue with three simultaneous identities:

- a young adult of the Lindquist line;
- a member of a family whose ancestors survived through preparation;
- a person from a lineage repeatedly treated as expendable.

---

## 3. The Lindquist position

The Lindquists are not simply comic outsiders. Their reputation has four layers:

1. **Useful:** they volunteer for exploration and remember the Studio routes and stories.
2. **Embarrassing:** their rituals, vocabulary and vinyl traditions are treated as strange.
3. **Untrusted:** they are rarely included in strategic decisions.
4. **Available:** dangerous missions are routinely suggested to them first.

The family itself interprets this history differently. Some Lindquists see each mission as a sacred continuation of Kaylee's promise. Others understand that the community has turned their belief into a supply of acceptable losses.

The Hero has grown up inside this contradiction. The mission is both an honour and an exploitation.

---

## 4. Julien and Marie before departure

Julien and Marie must be established as people before they become expedition members.

### Julien

Julien is an enthusiastic believer in the Lindquist tradition. He wants the Studio to be real because its existence would validate:

- Kaylee's final promise;
- the family pilgrimage tradition;
- the idea that the Lindquists have preserved a truth everyone else dismissed.

His confidence is genuine, but it also creates pressure on the Hero. He treats hesitation as a betrayal of the family.

### Marie

Marie is the practical member of the team. She agrees to go because she does not trust the mission's assumptions and does not want Julien and the Hero to be sent without someone capable of questioning them.

She understands the expedition's official rules better than the family mythology. Her presence makes the mission less reckless, but not safe.

### The three-person group

Before leaving, the team has a clear internal tension:

- Julien wants confirmation;
- Marie wants evidence;
- the Hero wants to know whether the family story means anything at all.

They share a family name and a destination, but not the same reason for going.

---

## 5. Why the mission is authorised

The mission is not authorised only because the Lindquists ask for it.

The settlement is under expansion pressure. The caves are full, and the Council has no low-risk way to create new living space. A possible location outside the known routes becomes politically valuable even though the evidence is weak.

The argument for the mission is:

- the location matches several old Lindquist descriptions;
- the route is within the ordinary four-hour operational window, if the map is accurate;
- a viable refuge could relieve pressure on the caves;
- the community needs new information even if the site is unusable;
- the Lindquist family has the strongest motivation to follow the lead.

The argument against it is:

- the location has never been verified;
- the map is based on inherited material, not a recent survey;
- the team may be selected because its losses are politically acceptable;
- surface expeditions have a history of incomplete returns;
- a failed mission could deepen the conflict between the Council and the Lindquists.

Sylvain Marchand supports the mission as a controlled reconnaissance, not as a promise of settlement.

---

## 6. Mission rules before departure

The team receives a deliberately limited mandate:

1. verify whether the location exists;
2. inspect the site only if the route remains viable;
3. record maps, documents and reusable equipment;
4. return before the ordinary four-hour limit;
5. abandon the mission if any member develops symptoms;
6. do not establish a permanent surface presence;
7. do not treat an unverified anomaly as protection.

The rules are repeated because the community knows that family mythology can override operational discipline.

No one gives the Hero a special exception. No one knows that one might become necessary.

---

## 7. Equipment and preparation

The expedition carries only what the community can justify losing:

- route maps based on Marchand and Lindquist records;
- water;
- compact food;
- basic medical supplies;
- route markers;
- hand tools;
- measuring and recording equipment;
- a return signal;
- controlled sound equipment for Hush-2 management;
- protective clothing suitable for a short surface excursion.

The equipment does not protect against the Filter. It only supports navigation, Hush-2 management, documentation and the possibility of return.

The most important preparation is not an object. It is the time discipline:

```text
leave
→ reach the target
→ inspect only what can be inspected safely
→ turn back before the window closes
```

The team knows the rule. The player knows the rule. The tragedy has not happened yet.

---

## 8. The final passage

The last sequence of the prologue moves through spaces the community knows well:

1. family quarters and final farewells;
2. equipment inspection at the Entrance Hall;
3. the controlled route through the working chambers;
4. the last familiar marker near the protected depth;
5. the ascent through older quarry passages;
6. the final protected threshold;
7. the opening toward the surface;
8. the Hero's first step outside.

The known world ends before the player receives any confirmation about Studio Echo.

The final emotional state is not discovery. It is commitment.

The player has learned:

- what the community is protecting;
- what the community is willing to risk;
- what the Lindquists believe;
- what Julien hopes to prove;
- what Marie doubts;
- what the Hero may lose by leaving.

Then the Hero steps outside.

---

## Deliberately unknown at the end of the prologue

The player must not yet know:

- whether the Hero is Resilient;
- whether Studio Echo still exists;
- whether a Crystal is present;
- whether the route is safe;
- whether the inherited map is accurate;
- what happened to the last Lindquist expedition;
- whether the community's account of Kaylee is reliable.

These are discoveries for the adventure, not facts of the starting exposition.

---

## Remaining canon questions

These questions are intentionally not resolved by this draft:

- the exact names of Julien and Marie's families;
- the exact form of the return signal;
- the identity of the expedition medic or equipment keeper;
- the exact Council procedure that authorises the mission;
- whether the Hero volunteers, is nominated or is pressured into accepting;
- the precise physical location of the last protected threshold;
- which parts of the inherited map are reliable.

They should be decided from the needs of the opening sequence, not filled with arbitrary detail.
