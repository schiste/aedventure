# Community classification framework

> Working framework for the Aedventure community generator. This page translates established research frameworks into a structured design vocabulary for fictional communities. It is not a universal scientific taxonomy and does not turn generated material into canon.

## Purpose

Aedventure communities should be generated as social systems rather than as collections of random character traits. A profile must explain how a population survives, governs itself, remembers its past, allocates risk and relates to other groups.

The framework combines four complementary perspectives:

- **Social-Ecological Systems (SES):** resources, resource units, governance systems and users, connected to the wider social, economic, political and ecological context.
- **Community resilience:** infrastructure, institutions, health, wellbeing, social capacity and economic means of adaptation.
- **Social identity:** membership, belonging, boundaries, status, subgroups and intergroup conflict.
- **Cultural values:** security, authority, autonomy, trust, solidarity, tolerance, technology and openness to change.

These perspectives are inputs to a generator, not an automatic classification of real people. A fictional profile must retain its sources, assumptions, contradictions and generation seed.

## Profile contract

Every generated community profile should contain:

- a stable identifier;
- a generation seed;
- a period and location;
- values with units where applicable;
- a status for each assertion: `canonical`, `reported`, `draft`, `disputed`, `inferred` or `unknown`;
- the source or design reason for each non-trivial value;
- explicit dependencies between variables;
- generated consequences separated from source facts;
- unresolved contradictions kept visible.

A generator may propose a community. It must not silently add that community to the canon, alter shared dates or invent a source.

## Dimension 1: Environment and material conditions

This dimension describes the setting that constrains every later choice.

| Variable | Questions for the profile | Possible consequences |
|---|---|---|
| Refuge type | Cave, mine, metro, bunker, industrial site, mixed network? | Architecture, mobility, available tools and collective memory |
| Refuge depth | How deep is the inhabited core? Where are the openings? | Filter protection, ventilation, travel routes and exposure risk |
| Geology | Limestone, granite, salt, unstable ground, flooded galleries? | Expansion cost, collapses, water access and Crystal plausibility |
| Climate and surface terrain | What surrounds the refuge? | Expedition routes, seasonal access and preserved resources |
| Resource system | What can be produced, recycled or recovered locally? | Diet, labour, trade and reasons to risk the surface |
| Resource units | Which concrete units are scarce: medicine, fuel, parts, food, tools? | Conflicts, rationing and expedition priorities |
| Infrastructure | What survived from before the Silence? | Technical capacity, repair culture and strategic vulnerability |
| Acoustic environment | What sounds are available underground? | Hush-2 continuity, sleep practices, work rhythms and taboos |
| Hazard profile | Collapse, flooding, creatures, rival groups, disease? | Guard duty, settlement layout and political legitimacy |

## Dimension 2: Population and demography

Population variables should describe a distribution, not just a headcount.

| Variable | Questions for the profile | Possible consequences |
|---|---|---|
| Population size | How many people live in the community? | Specialisation, redundancy and political scale |
| Age distribution | Children, adults, elders and missing generations? | Education, succession and labour pressure |
| Family structure | Households, clans, communal care, adoption? | Inheritance, belonging and conflict resolution |
| Origin diversity | One founding group or several arrivals? | Languages, memories, status groups and competing traditions |
| Resilient proportion | Are Resilients present, rare, hidden or politically controlled? | Exploration, stigma, privilege and exploitation |
| Health profile | Chronic illness, trauma, disability, inherited conditions? | Care systems, work allocation and social status |
| Skill distribution | Which skills are common, rare or lost? | Bottlenecks, apprenticeship and dependency on individuals |
| Generational distance | How many generations have lived without outside contact? | Mythologised history, language drift and technological loss |
| Internal mobility | Can people change households, jobs or status? | Social cohesion, escape routes and faction formation |

## Dimension 3: Governance and institutions

Governance is not only the name of the leader. It is the set of rules that allocates authority, work, information and punishment.

| Variable | Questions for the profile | Possible consequences |
|---|---|---|
| Authority structure | Council, household elders, technical guild, religious authority, militia? | Decision speed, legitimacy and succession |
| Decision process | Consensus, vote, lottery, command, ritual or opaque procedure? | Crisis response and minority protection |
| Rule source | Written code, precedent, oral law, engineering necessity? | Adaptability and disputes over interpretation |
| Sanctions | Exile, ration loss, confinement, labour, restitution, violence? | Fear, compliance and resistance |
| Leadership succession | Election, inheritance, achievement, revelation, force? | Stability, coups and generational conflict |
| Resource allocation | Rationing, market, communal pool, patronage, status rights? | Inequality, black markets and solidarity |
| Loss governance | Who authorises dangerous expeditions and accepts casualties? | Responsibility, martyrdom narratives and dissent |
| Knowledge governance | Open archives, guarded expertise, ritual secrets, lost records? | Innovation, misinformation and power concentration |
| Foreigners and returnees | Guests, traders, threats, adoptees, impurity risks? | Diplomacy, quarantine and identity boundaries |
| Justice and repair | Punishment, compensation, mediation, public confession? | Feuds, trust and reintegration |

## Dimension 4: Culture, identity and meaning

A community separated from the wider world for centuries will not merely preserve an old culture. It will reinterpret it.

| Variable | Questions for the profile | Possible consequences |
|---|---|---|
| Collective identity | What sentence explains who “we” are? | Inclusion, pride and propaganda |
| Membership boundary | Birth, oath, work, faith, origin, residence, adoption? | Citizenship, exclusion and conversion |
| Founding narrative | What event does the community treat as its beginning? | Heroes, blame and ritual anniversaries |
| Memory of the old world | Archive, taboo, nostalgia, contempt, practical reference? | Education and attitudes to ruins |
| Religion or cosmology | Divine punishment, natural process, ancestral duty, unknown? | Rituals, authority and Crystal interpretation |
| Sound relationship | Necessary tool, sacred practice, nuisance, weapon, private comfort? | Hush-2 routines and cultural symbolism |
| Silence relationship | Enemy, discipline, sacred state, research subject? | Training, punishment and political movements |
| Crystal relationship | Resource, ancestor, machine, miracle, contagion, rival claim? | Secrecy, pilgrimage, science and war |
| Taboo system | Which acts threaten survival or identity? | Social control and dramatic transgression |
| Language and symbols | What words, signs, names and records have changed? | Misunderstanding, hidden kinship and regional identity |
| Art and ceremony | How are grief, birth, work and risk commemorated? | Cohesion, propaganda and historical distortion |

## Dimension 5: Relations, strategy and change

This dimension describes how the community acts beyond its immediate refuge.

| Variable | Questions for the profile | Possible consequences |
|---|---|---|
| External contact | None, rumours, messengers, trade, regular diplomacy? | Information quality and cultural divergence |
| Communication capacity | What can travel: voice, drums, runners, records, machines? | Coordination range and misunderstanding |
| Trade position | Self-sufficient, dependent, indispensable, isolated? | Bargaining power, scarcity and alliances |
| Diplomatic posture | Open, cautious, predatory, missionary, fragmented? | Border incidents and coalition formation |
| Threat perception | What does the group fear most, and why? | Defence spending and scapegoats |
| Expedition protocol | Who goes, with what equipment, for which resource and under whose authority? | Risk distribution, expertise and class formation |
| Crystal strategy | Hide, study, share, control, worship, destroy, ignore? | Surface expansion and Crystal conflicts |
| Innovation capacity | Experimentation allowed, controlled, forbidden or accidental? | Recovery speed and internal dissent |
| Adaptation speed | How quickly can rules change after a failure? | Resilience versus tradition |
| Secrecy policy | What is hidden from members, neighbours and future generations? | Espionage, myth and delayed catastrophe |
| Conflict history | What unresolved losses still shape policy? | Feuds, memorials and revenge cycles |
| Future horizon | Restoration, survival, expansion, transcendence, resignation? | Long-term projects and generational sacrifice |

## Dimension 6: Technology and production

Technology should be modelled as a maintained social capability, not as a list of artefacts.

| Variable | Questions for the profile | Possible consequences |
|---|---|---|
| Energy system | What produces, stores and distributes power? | Work rhythms, inequality and failure modes |
| Manufacturing | What can be repaired, copied or only recovered? | Bottlenecks, trade and strategic secrecy |
| Medical capability | Which treatments, diagnostics and procedures remain? | Life expectancy, fertility, disability and expedition risk |
| Food system | What is cultivated, preserved, hunted or traded? | Diet, labour, resilience and population ceiling |
| Water system | How is water found, treated, stored and rationed? | Settlement size, hygiene and political control |
| Acoustic technology | Which generators, instruments and dampening tools exist? | Hush-2 continuity, exploration and cultural practice |
| Data and archives | What records survive and who can read them? | Historical accuracy, education and legitimacy |
| Transport | What can move people and resources through the refuge? | Trade radius, rescue capacity and isolation |
| Maintenance culture | Who keeps systems working and how is expertise transferred? | Fragility, apprenticeship and succession |

## Dimension 7: Health and life course

Health variables connect the population model to lived experience.

| Variable | Questions for the profile | Possible consequences |
|---|---|---|
| Baseline mortality | What kills people without a major crisis? | Population growth and age structure |
| Infant and maternal mortality | What are the risks of birth and early childhood? | Family size, care institutions and grief rituals |
| Disease burden | Which endemic, infectious or inherited conditions exist? | Quarantine, stigma and labour capacity |
| Hush-2 management | How is continuous sound maintained and who bears the burden? | Work rotations, disability and political power |
| Resilient health | How are Resilients identified, treated and protected? | Exploitation, privilege and genetic memory |
| Injury and exposure | Which accidents and expeditions are survivable? | Disability, compensation and taboo |
| Mental health | How are trauma, isolation and silence treated? | Social cohesion, ritual and exclusion |
| Reproductive policy | Is reproduction private, communal, regulated or forbidden? | Growth rate, family conflict and succession |
| Life expectancy | What differs by status, work, sex, age or exposure? | Elders, knowledge transfer and political authority |
| Care network | Who cares for children, sick people and injured workers? | Solidarity, household structure and abandonment |

## Dimension 8: History and temporal change

A community must be generated as a trajectory, not a frozen description.

| Variable | Questions for the profile | Possible consequences |
|---|---|---|
| Founding state | What was true when the community formed? | Starting constraints and inherited institutions |
| Current state | What is true at the present date? | Current conflicts, resources and opportunities |
| Turning points | Which deaths, discoveries, schisms or contacts changed the group? | Institutional change and competing memories |
| Crisis history | Which crises reduced or reorganised the population? | Trauma, risk policy and demographic scars |
| Contact history | When did communication start, stop or resume? | Knowledge drift and false histories |
| Migration history | Who joined, left, was expelled or was absorbed? | Identity, language and property claims |
| Technology history | Which capabilities were gained, lost or rediscovered? | Uneven development and old ruins |
| Crystal history | When was a Crystal found, lost, hidden or contested? | Surface access, religion and diplomacy |
| Memory conflict | Which versions of history compete? | Factions, archives and unreliable testimony |
| Expected future | What does the community think will happen next? | Projects, sacrifices and political legitimacy |

## Temporal state model

The generator must require two snapshots for every mature community profile:

```text
starting_state
  -> dated transitions and causal events
  -> current_state
```

`starting_state` describes the community at its founding or at the beginning of the selected narrative period. `current_state` describes the community at the chosen present date. Both snapshots use the same eight dimension families so that changes can be compared rather than merely narrated.

A transition should identify:

- the date or date range;
- the affected dimensions;
- the trigger;
- the observed change;
- the source or generation rationale;
- the people who benefited or lost status;
- the uncertainty and competing interpretations.

Agents may write the history of a transition, but the transition itself remains structured data.

## Demographic credibility model

Population is a trajectory with uncertainty, not a decorative number. The first model should be period-based and expose at least:

- starting population and age structure;
- births and fertility assumptions;
- ordinary mortality;
- infant and maternal mortality;
- disease and accident shocks;
- carrying capacity from food, water, energy and space;
- migration, mergers and splits;
- Crystal-related changes in safe area or production;
- confidence range and source status.

The current figure of approximately two million people in 311 AS is retained as a **high working hypothesis**, not as canon. Existing checkpoints must be tested against the model before being promoted:

| Checkpoint | Existing working estimate | Status |
|---|---:|---|
| Day 1 | ~400,000 | `draft`, inherited estimate |
| Year 1 AS | ~120,000 | `draft`, inherited estimate |
| Year 50 AS | ~200,000 | `draft`, inherited estimate |
| Year 200 AS | ~800,000 | `draft`, inherited estimate |
| Year 250 AS | ~1,200,000 | `draft`, inherited estimate |
| Year 290 AS | ~1,800,000 | `draft`, inherited estimate |
| Year 311 AS | ~2,000,000 | `draft`, high hypothesis |

The first regional calibration target is Paris and a 1,000 km radius. It uses a spatial grid or distance bands and separates official, military, industrial, mining, transport, private and improvised refuges. It estimates reachable survivors rather than merely adding published shelter capacities.

The model must not hide the implied average growth rate. From 120,000 in Year 1 to two million in Year 311, the existing checkpoints imply roughly 0.91% annual growth on average. That may be possible under favourable conditions, but it requires explicit assumptions about fertility, mortality, carrying capacity and migration.


The generator should produce consequences from combinations, not from isolated rolls.

Examples:

- **Deep refuge + long isolation + scarce medicine + technical council** can produce strict apprenticeship, controlled expeditions and a political class built around maintenance expertise.
- **Mixed origins + inherited founding trauma + closed membership** can produce competing genealogies and a ritualised distinction between founders and later arrivals.
- **Rare Resilients + dangerous routes + authoritarian governance** can produce privileged scouts, coerced service or an underground movement against exploitation.
- **Crystal discovered by an isolated religious community** can produce a sacred interpretation that is internally coherent even when its physical explanation is wrong.
- **Regular contact lost for three centuries** can produce incompatible protocols, false shared history and communities that no longer recognise the same symbols.

The generator must record these as derived consequences, not as facts that were present in the input profile.

## Generation pipeline

```text
Profile and seed
  -> constraints and dependencies
  -> institutions and daily practices
  -> internal groups and unresolved tensions
  -> external relationships
  -> historical events and turning points
  -> agent-generated story proposals
  -> consistency and source validation
  -> Markdown draft with status metadata
```

Agents may generate:

- founding stories;
- customs and rituals;
- faction histories;
- expedition accounts;
- Crystal discovery narratives;
- disputes between generations;
- diplomatic encounters;
- contradictory testimonies.

Agents must not:

- promote a generated event to canon;
- invent a citation;
- overwrite a structured value without a decision;
- erase contradictions to make a story cleaner;
- assume that two isolated communities use the same concepts;
- turn a plausible consequence into a historical fact.

## Recommended structured model

The eventual data record should separate inputs from generated material:

```json
{
  "id": "community.example",
  "seed": 12345,
  "starting_state": {
    "period": {},
    "dimensions": {}
  },
  "current_state": {
    "period": {},
    "dimensions": {}
  },
  "transitions": [],
  "dimensions": {
    "environment": {},
    "population": {},
    "governance": {},
    "culture": {},
    "relations": {},
    "technology": {},
    "health": {},
    "history": {}
  },
  "constraints": [],
  "derived_consequences": [],
  "story_proposals": [],
  "sources": [],
  "status": "draft"
}
```

Numeric values such as population, depth, survival windows and dates must use the shared registries under `lore/data/`. The generator should reference stable IDs rather than copy those numbers into several profiles.

## Research basis and limits

The SES framework is useful because it explicitly separates resource systems, resource units, governance systems and users, while connecting them to broader social, economic, political and ecological settings. The community resilience literature provides a multidimensional view of infrastructure, institutions, health, social capacity and economic adaptation. Social identity research helps model belonging, status and intergroup conflict. The World Values Survey provides empirical value dimensions and questionnaires, but its data should not be treated as a universal classifier for fictional, isolated communities.

These frameworks describe analytical dimensions. They do not predict a community's exact behaviour and they do not remove the need for situated history, local sources and editorial judgment.
